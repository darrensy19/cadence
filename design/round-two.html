Border-trace — what it is

A thin line of light travels once around the card's border over the length of the interval — literal analog progress instead of a number or bar. It replaces the pip row entirely; the border is the progress.

Border-trace — the risk

Needs to read as an arc at both ends of the length range — a 5-minute break and a 50-minute focus block — not just at the 32-minute mark shown here. The reveal uses a Chromium-only masking trick (mask-composite: exclude), consistent with the rest of this project's Chromium-only stance.

Breathing card — what it is

The glass card itself scales and glows on a slow ~4s cycle while running, stopping dead the instant you pause — the same freeze rule js/pulse.js used to enforce, now applied to the whole card instead of a waveform.

Breathing card — the risk

A moving card is a bigger visual commitment than a moving line or a blinking colon. If it reads as busy rather than calm against a wallpaper that's already warm, it's the first of the three to drop.

Just the clock — what it is

No dedicated motion element. The only movement is the seconds colon blinking and the accent colour crossfading between focus (amber) and break (teal) on a state change — closest to Quiet's restraint. The wallpaper supplies the warmth; the clock supplies the only motion.

Just the clock — the risk

Might read as too quiet once it's paired with a full wallpaper and a glass card — both of the other two variants exist partly because a plain ticking clock didn't feel alive enough against Pulse's flat void. Worth sitting with this one for a real ninety minutes, not just a glance, before assuming restraint is automatically right here.
