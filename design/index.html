A · Quiet — what it is

The time is the interface. Everything else sits at about 30% opacity and only comes up when you move the mouse — hover the mock to see it. No wallpaper, no player on screen, no log.

Best if you want the app to disappear while you work. Costs you the ambient side you asked for — music would live behind a keystroke, not on screen.

A · the risk

Restraint this severe only works if the typography is genuinely good, and the system font stack differs between your MacBook and the Windows laptop. This direction is the one that most needs a self-hosted typeface.

B · Station — what it is

The wallpaper is the app: a slow animated gradient with film grain, content floating in glass above it. The YouTube player is a real element, bottom left, which is what YouTube's embed terms require anyway.

Best if you want the flocus feeling — atmosphere, something nice to look at — with the clutter removed.

B · the risk

Ambience competes with focus. Movement in your peripheral vision for ninety minutes is a real cost, so the drift here is deliberately slow — and it should probably stop entirely during a focus block and resume on breaks.

The gradient is generated, so it costs no bytes. Your own wallpapers would be read from a local folder.

C · Console — what it is

An instrument. Categories down the left with today's hours against each, timer in the middle, today's log on the right. Nothing is hidden and nothing moves.

Best if you want to see where the day went while you are still in it — closest in spirit to your existing trackers, and it carries their accent colour.

C · the risk

It is the least calm of the three, and a visible log invites you to watch the number instead of working. It also has the least obvious home for music and wallpaper — they would be a mode you switch into rather than the default view.
